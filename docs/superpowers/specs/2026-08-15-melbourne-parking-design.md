# ParkAlong — Approved Product Design

## Product

An iOS 17+ SwiftUI app opens directly on a map of central Melbourne. A driver can use current location or MapKit destination search, select a stay of 15 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, or 3+ hours, compare legally suitable zones by trustworthy live vacancies, inspect vacant bays and restriction details, and hand the chosen coordinate to Apple Maps.

The app has no account, backend, reservation, payment, Google Maps, or runtime AI. City of Melbourne and Apple services are the only network destinations. Melbourne CBD remains usable when location is denied.

## Data and trust

`ParkingAPIClient` builds Opendatasoft v2.1 requests, decodes their real GeoJSON-shaped records, and follows explicit offset pagination. Sensor rows more than 24 hours old, missing a usable zone/coordinate, or not reporting `Present`/`Unoccupied` are discarded. `AvailabilityEngine` groups qualifying rows by zone and reports free/total counts. Individual free-bay coordinates are retained only for the selected zone.

Stable zone/street metadata and compact historical aggregates are bundled JSON artifacts generated by reproducible scripts. The latest network result is cached briefly; expired data is never relabelled live. Refresh runs after destination changes, foreground transitions, manual action, and on a roughly two-minute cadence. A feed failure or broadly stale response switches the presentation to bundled typical availability with explicit wording.

## Restrictions

`RestrictionEngine` evaluates Australia/Melbourne weekday and local time, including intervals crossing midnight. Public QP, P, MP, and FP rules are supported. Loading, permit, disability, no-parking, clearway, and ambiguous rules are excluded. Duration is a hard filter. Paid/free is stated only when determinable; street signs remain authoritative.

## Prediction and ranking

The offline Python generator streams the 2019 archive without loading it into memory and aggregates normalized street segment, weekday, and 15-minute bucket into occupied ratio, turnover, and sample count. `PredictionEngine` heavily weights live occupancy for short ETAs (80% at 15 minutes), tapers the weight with longer ETAs, clamps estimates to the trusted bay count, and exposes conservative human copy and confidence.

Eligible zones are ranked deterministically: 70% predicted available spaces, 20% inverse walking distance, and 10% freshness/confidence. The most available option must beat a closer materially worse option.

## UI

The interface is map-first, premium native iOS: a compact floating search control, an integrated stay selector, high-contrast availability pins, a visually distinct Best bet, and a focused material bottom sheet rather than dashboard card grids. The sheet shows street segment, availability, active restriction, paid/free status where known, prediction, walking distance, freshness/confidence, navigation, and the signs-govern disclaimer. Accessibility labels, Dynamic Type, light/dark contrast, reduced-motion awareness, safe areas, and VoiceOver order are first-class.

Deterministic fixtures activated only by UI-test launch arguments cover live, loading, error, stale/typical, permission-denied, search, selection, duration, refresh, and navigation interception states without introducing fake data to the normal live flow.

## Verification

Unit tests cover decoding, pagination, grouping, freshness, restrictions and time boundaries, duration eligibility, prediction, ranking, and cache expiry. Repository tests use fixtures; a separate smoke command proves the live City API contract. UI tests drive the simulator flows. Final proof includes a clean build, all unit/UI tests, a real-API manual run at Melbourne coordinates, navigation URL interception, and inspected screenshots for map, search, selected zone, loading/error, light, and dark appearances.
